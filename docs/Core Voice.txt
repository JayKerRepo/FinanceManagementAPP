## Voice interaction examples

### 1. Expense management

#### Quick expense entry
- User: "I just spent $45 on gas at Shell"
- Agent: "Got it! $45 for gas at Shell. Which business should I charge this to?"
- User: "Business Alpha"
- Agent: "Perfect! I've added $45 for gas at Shell to Business Alpha. Anything else?"

#### Multi-item expense
- User: "I bought office supplies today - $89 for printer paper, $45 for pens, and $120 for a new monitor"
- Agent: "I've added three expenses: $89 for printer paper, $45 for pens, and $120 for a monitor. Should I categorize them all as Office & Admin?"
- User: "Yes, that's right"
- Agent: "Done! All three expenses are saved under Office & Admin for today."

#### Expense with context
- User: "Add a $250 expense for client lunch"
- Agent: "I've added $250 for client lunch. Should I categorize this as Travel & Meals and mark it as client entertainment for tax purposes?"
- User: "Yes, please"
- Agent: "Perfect! I've added the tax tag. This will help with your deductions."

#### Expense correction
- User: "Actually, that gas expense was $50, not $45"
- Agent: "No problem! I've updated the Shell expense from $45 to $50. Anything else to change?"
- User: "Change the date to yesterday"
- Agent: "Done! Updated the date to yesterday."

#### Bulk expense entry
- User: "I have several expenses from my trip: $120 for hotel, $45 for meals, $89 for parking, and $250 for flight"
- Agent: "I've added all four expenses. Should I categorize them all as Travel & Meals, or would you like to split them differently?"
- User: "Put the flight and hotel under Travel, meals under Travel & Meals, and parking under Other"
- Agent: "Perfect! I've categorized them accordingly. Your trip expenses total $504."
